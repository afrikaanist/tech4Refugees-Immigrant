Research & public work

Evidence, ideas and community experience.

Tech4RIM grows from founder Afrikaan Sahra’s long-standing work in refugee advocacy, youth education, digital inclusion and community storytelling. This page identifies both founder-led work and projects formally sponsored by Tech4Refugees or Tech4RIM.

Community work

The work began before the organization had its current name.

Earlier activities are presented as part of Afrikaan Sahra’s founder journey. Later entries name Tech4Refugees or Tech4RIM when the organization served as the program lead or project sponsor.

2015
Founder-led work · Kakuma, Kenya

Kakuma Youth Leaders and Youth Voices of Kakuma

Afrikaan organized approximately 60 young people around translation support, community activities and youth connection. With other young leaders, Afrikaan also helped create Youth Voices of Kakuma to document refugee talent, skills and daily life through digital storytelling.

Read the documented founder interview ↗
2016
Founder-led work · Geneva, Switzerland

Refugee youth advocacy

Afrikaan participated in international refugee-youth advocacy, speaking about the proposed closure of Kakuma and Dadaab refugee camps, forced return and the importance of including refugee youth in decisions about their futures.

Read the documented founder interview ↗
2018
Founder-led work · Seattle

Digital skills and youth STEM learning

Afrikaan delivered basic computer-skills classes for East African adults and led summer learning with the Somali Youth and Family Club, teaching mathematics, English and coding to middle- and high-school students.

Read the program history ↗
2020
Tech4Refugees project

More Than a Refugee campaign and digital learning

Tech4Refugees used storytelling to highlight the skills and talents of people living in refugee camps. In a 2020 interview, Afrikaan also described mentoring young people in Kenya and Uganda and supporting computer-based learning in Kakuma.

Read the Team Gather interview ↗
2021
Founder-led collaboration · Seattle University

COVID-19 Civility Pledge

Afrikaan helped develop a youth-centered campaign promoting community responsibility, informed vaccination decisions and safer practices, with particular attention to communities experiencing disproportionate pandemic harms.

Read the Alfie Scholars feature ↗
2024
Tech4Refugees organizational project

Seattle University resource-navigation capstone

Tech4Refugees served as a community sponsor for a Seattle University computer-science capstone team developing a multilingual concept for navigating social services. The prototype remains a learning asset for future community testing and development.

Read Seattle University’s project feature ↗
